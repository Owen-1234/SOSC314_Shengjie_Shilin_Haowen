Part 1. Research Question and Motivation

Rather than documenting reality, news reporting actively constructs social meaning thereby shaping audience perceptions of problem definition and causal attribution [1]. In climate-related felds, empirical work demonstrates that media framing influences public risk perception and attitudes toward policy debates [2]. Moreover, national media systems routinely embed climate issues within distinct political, economic, and developmental contexts[3]. Accordingly, our aim is not to adjudicate normative claims about accuracy or legitimacy, but to leverage large-scale textual data to identify stable, replicable patterns in media discourse.

Therefore, this study maily focus on the following research questions:

(1) Do Chinese and Western media frame the topic of “energy transition” differently?
(2) Do these framing patterns exhibit temporal trends across the 2020–2025 period?

Specifically, Drawing on Grimmer and Stewart’s [4] methodology, we employ topic modeling to uncover latent, interpretable themes thereby revealing focal points in media attention to energy and climate governance.

Part 2. Data

We constructed a curated corpus of over 7,000 news articles (Chinese Media more than 5000, Western Media more than 2000) published between 2020 and 2025. Articles were retrieved via targeted web crawling from four nationally representative outlets: “China Daily”, “People’s Daily” (Chinese media), “The Guardian” and “Reuters” (Western media). Inclusion required explicit mention of at least one of three key qords: “energy transition,” “carbon neutrality,” or “climate policy”. For every document, we retained four structured metadata fields: geographic origin (China vs. Western), publication year, specific outlet, and the full main text.

To ensure analytical rigor, we applied standardized preprocessing procedures: removal of duplicate articles, elimination of boilerplate webpage elements (e.g., navigation bars, advertisements), filtering of high-frequency function words (stop words), normalization of punctuation and whitespace, and exclusion of terms appearing in fewer than five documents. Nevertheless, we explicitly acknowledge some limitations: editorial discretion inevitably introduces data bias into news selection and presentation, and unequal sampling volumes across outlets constrain direct frequency-based comparisons. Consequently, our findings characterize media discourse not public opinion—and reflect associative patterns rather than causal relationships.

Part 3: Problem Setup

3.1 Machine Learning Task

The primary machine learning task for this study is unsupervised probabilistic topic modeling [4]. Our objective is to uncover thematic structures within a corpus of over 7,000 news articles concerning the global energy transition [5].

3.2 Target Variables

Because this is an unsupervised learning task, there is no traditional target variable to predict. Instead, the model aims to estimate two unobserved latent variables [6]:

  • Document-Topic Proportions (θ\thetaθ): The continuous probability distribution of topics within each specific document.
  • Topic-Word Distributions (β\betaβ): The probability distribution over the entire vocabulary conditioned on a specific topic.

3.3 Inputs and Features

The model ingests two parallel streams of input data:

  1. Textual Features: The primary input is a Document-Term Matrix (DTM). To isolate the immediate conceptual environment, we extracted all tokens within a 20-word radius of each seed term ("energy transition," "carbon neutrality," and "climate policy").
  2. Document-Level Covariates: We input specific metadata for each document to structuralize the topic priors:
  • Categorical Covariates: The media region ("China" vs. "Western"), the specific news source, and the search keyword.
  • Continuous Covariate: The publication year (2020–2025), utilized to track longitudinal narrative shifts.

3.4 Assumptions

We assume that document metadata dictates topical prevalence [7]. Theoretically, we hypothesize that Chinese media focuses on international cooperation (e.g., the Belt and Road Initiative and China-Africa partnerships), whereas Western media prioritizes ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) criteria.

Part 4: Methods

4.1 Baseline Models

Initially, we established a baseline using a Weighted Frequency Analysis, which calculated the frequency of each term per 1,000 articles. This approach standardized the density of the words and adjusted for the imbalanced sample size between Chinese and UK documents.

For the probabilistic modeling phase, we employed Dirichlet-Multinomial Regression (DMR) [8] as our primary structural baseline. While DMR successfully allowed covariates to influence topic priors, it imposed strict linear constraints on continuous variables. Given our hypothesis that climate narratives exhibit event-driven volatility, DMR was deemed insufficient for our final temporal analysis.

4.2 Final Model Selection and Justification

The final inferential framework selected for substantive interpretation is the Structural Topic Model (STM) [7] with an optimal granularity of K=20K = 20K=20.

STM was chosen because it addresses the limitations of DMR by allowing continuous variables to be modeled flexibly. To operationalize our assumption of non-linear temporal dynamics, we specified the prevalence model utilizing spline functions:

4.3 Feature Engineering and Text Representation

Instead of analyzing full articles, we applied a 20-word sliding window extraction method around our core seed terms to isolate the immediate conceptual environment. To reduce noise, we systematically removed numbers, standard punctuation, and generic stopwords. Crucially, we disabled stemming (stem = FALSE) during the text processing phase. This ensured that specific political and technological terminologies retained their exact original forms, preventing the over-collapsing of distinct semantic nuances.

4.4 Training and Tuning Approach

Our hyperparameter tuning primarily focused on optimizing the number of latent topics (KKK). We evaluated candidate models at K=10K = 10K=10, 202020, and 303030. The K=10K = 10K=10 specification produced overly broad categories, conflating distinct sectors like wind and coal, whereas K=30K = 30K=30 captured excessive website boilerplate and noise. Consequently, we selected K=20K = 20K=20 to balance statistical robustness with thematic interpretability. To ensure stability and reproducibility, the STM was trained utilizing deterministic Spectral Initialization [9], with the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm capped at 75 iterations. Finally, semantic validity was confirmed through manual labeling using FREX and Lift metrics [10].

4.5 Model Outputs

Upon convergence, the STM generated three core outputs that form the empirical foundation of our analysis:

  • Topical Content (K20_top_words.txt): Extracted top terms for each topic enabled us to interpret semantic clusters and assign qualitative labels, such as "Renewables & Hydrogen".
  • Document-Level Proportions (K20_theta.csv): A continuous θ\thetaθ matrix detailed the exact topic mixture for each article, allowing for micro-level tracking of narratives.
  • Aggregated Prevalences (K20_mean_theta.csv): Grouping θ\thetaθ by metadata covariates allowed us to statistically quantify regional narrative divergence (Δ\DeltaΔ) and map temporal trends from 2020 to 2025.

Part 5. Results and Evaluation

5.1 Evaluation Strategy

Our evaluation focuses on whether the model produces coherent, interpretable, and substantively meaningful thematic structures that illuminate cross-regional differences in climate discourse.

Specifically, we assess model performance along three dimensions:

  1. Pre-model descriptive validation — whether localized keyword contexts reveal systematic differences prior to modeling (using a 20-word context window analysis).
  2. Model-based Structural differentiation — whether STM-derived topic proportions (θ) exhibit statistically significant cross-regional divergence.
  3. Model-based Temporal coherence — whether topic prevalence evolves in structured, event-aligned, or trend-consistent patterns rather than random fluctuation.

5.2 Preliminary Narrative Patterns: 20-Word Window Analysis

Before implementing STM model, we examined the 20-word window surrounding three key terms: energy transition, carbon neutrality, and climate policy.

In the Chinese corpus, frequently co-occurring words include terms such as “achieve,” “peak,” “development,” “implementation,” “global,” and “cooperation.” These terms cluster around forward-looking goals, national planning frameworks, and coordinated policy execution.

In the Western corpus, high-frequency contextual terms include references to “government,” political leaders such as “Morrison,” country names such as “China,” “Australia,” and fossil fuel sectors such as “gas” and “oil.”

5.3 Cross-Regional Topic Prevalence

We estimated an STM model with 20 topics and extracted document-level topic proportions (θ). To improve clarity, we manual generate provisional topic labels based on each topic’s highest-probability terms and representative documents. Mean topic prevalence was calculated separately for Chinese and Western media. To assess statistical differences, two-sample t-tests with Bonferroni correction were applied (p < 0.01 threshold). The cross-regional results indicate statistically significant differences across multiple topics.

Topics with Higher Prevalence in Western Media

The following topics show higher mean proportions in the Western corpus:

  • Activism & Media Boilerplate
  • Australian Politics
  • Coal & Emissions Policy
  • Oil & Gas Transition

These topics are characterized by vocabulary related to political actors, regulatory enforcement, fossil fuel transition conflict, and partisan debate. Rather than emphasizing implementation outcomes, the discourse centers on accountability, political actors, and institutional negotiation.

Topics with Higher Prevalence in Chinese Media

The following topics show higher mean proportions in the Chinese corpus:

  • Renewables & Hydrogen
  • Low-Carbon Policy
  • EVs & Batteries
  • CPC Governance
  • Expos & Tech Events

These themes highlight technological upgrading, industrial capacity building, institutional coordination, and policy roadmap execution. Climate transition is framed as a structured national project involving state guidance, industrial strategy, and technological modernization.

Shared Topics

Both corpora exhibit substantial attention to topics related to:

  • COP & Paris Talks

Although mean proportions vary slightly, this topic appears prominently in both datasets. This shared emphasis underscores the symbolic and diplomatic significance of international climate governance forums.

Part 6. Error Analysis / Diagnostics

The primary limitation of this study stems from the inherent constraints of topic modeling, particularly STM in capturing nuanced semantic phenomena. As a probabilistic model grounded in word co-occurrence statistics, STM operates under a bag-of-words assumption, which precludes sensitivity to linguistic features such as tone, evaluative stance, irony, or implicit causal reasoning. Consequently, while STM effectively estimates the statistical distribution of topics across documents, it lacks the capacity to infer argumentative strategies, normative commitments, or value-laden judgments [11].

In the model selection phase, we evaluated K=10K = 10K=10, 202020, and 303030 using three diagnostic metrics: held-out likelihood, residual sum of squares, and semantic coherence. Held-out likelihood improved monotonically with increasing K, whereas residuals declined substantially indicating that K=10K = 10K=10 led to structural underfitting and insufficient representation of corpus-level co-occurrence patterns. Conversely, semantic coherence deteriorated markedly beyond K=20K = 20K=20, signaling over-fragmentation: higher K values yielded statistically distinct but semantically incoherent and interpretively unstable topics [13].

Given this trade-off between statistical fit and substantive interpretability, K=20K = 20K=20 was selected—not as a globally optimal solution, but as an empirically justified compromise aligned with established guidelines for balancing model fidelity and human-readable topic structure [7].

Part 7. Interpretation and Substantive Takeaways

Our results reveal a consistent cross-regional divergence in how energy transition is framed. Chinese media predominantly portray it as a state-led developmental and strategic transformation, emphasizing policy implementation, national targets, technological innovation, and long-term planning. High-prevalence themes include renewable expansion, low-carbon governance, electric vehicles, and hydrogen development, producing a forward-looking, action-oriented narrative.

Western media, by contrast, more frequently frame energy transition as politically contested, highlighting partisan debate, fossil-fuel phase-out disputes, regulatory conflict, and sectoral interests, often embedded in electoral and international negotiation contexts. Although both regions attend to COP meetings and the Paris Agreement, Western coverage stresses negotiation and accountability, whereas Chinese reporting integrates these events into national strategy.

Temporally, discourse from 2020 to 2025 evolves through staged reweighting rather than linear change: early attention reflects uncertainty linked to energy security and post-pandemic recovery; after 2021, policy implementation and green industry expansion gain prominence; later narratives increasingly emphasize technological competition and industrial strategy.

Based on the empirical results, readers can conclude that:

  • Energy transition narratives differ across Chinese and Western media rather than varying randomly at the article level.
  • These differences are observable across multiple thematic clusters and are statistically significant.
  • Topic attention changes over time in patterned ways, reflecting both gradual evolution and episodic peaks.

Part 8. Limitations and Future Work

This study faces several structural constraints. First, the corpus draws exclusively from four major media outlets—two Chinese and two Western—limiting its representativeness of the broader public discourse ecosystem and introducing potential media selection bias. Moreover, the unequal sample sizes across regional sources may bias proportional topic estimates and attenuate cross-regional comparability. Second, the five-year temporal scope (2020–2025) captures policy-driven episodic events and recurring international forums yet remains insufficient for detecting long-term shifts in narrative architecture or deep-seated discursive evolution [4].

Methodologically, STM’s unsupervised nature restricts its analytical scope to lexical frequency distributions, rendering it insensitive to syntactic dependencies, contextual embeddings, or pragmatic functions. As such, it cannot detect sentiment polarity, normative framing, or logical entailment—only shifts in topical prevalence and attentional allocation [11].

To address these limitations, future work will expand the media sample to include diverse national, linguistic, and genre-based sources, extend the time series beyond 2025 to support longitudinal inference, and integrate transformer-based models—such as fine-tuned stance classifiers or framing detectors—to augment STM’s shallow semantic representations. This hybrid approach aims to move beyond descriptive topic tracking toward explanatory analysis of how energy transition narratives are constructed, contested, and legitimized across geopolitical contexts.
